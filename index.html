Approach

Approachable but premium. Fast but trustworthy. Global but unmistakably from somewhere.

The brands people love hold two opposing things at once, on purpose. The ones that struggle hold two things by accident — and can't tell which is winning. Most design work tries to resolve the tension. That's how you get products that are clear but forgettable, or distinctive but unusable. The better outcome is a tension held deliberately, made load-bearing. Naming the contradiction is most of the work. Designing something that holds it is the rest.

Origin

A merchant sells a spear that pierces any shield,
and a shield no spear
can pierce.

Someone asks what happens if you use one on the other. He has no answer. I do. My parents named me Lance Shields. Mujun, 矛盾, is the Japanese word for contradiction, and it comes from that parable. It traveled from China to Japan and became an everyday word. It never made it into English at all. Same story, three markets, three fates, which is the global brand problem in miniature and the work I've spent twenty years doing on both sides of the Pacific.
